## PODCASTS

**No Priors / Superintelligence Needs the Physical World with CEO and Co-Founder of Arda Bob McGrew**

**The Takeaway:** Automating researchers is a one-time S-curve, not recursive self-improvement; when hypotheses get cheap, wet labs, GPUs, and factories become the scarce complement, so superintelligence needs the physical world.

**一句话：** 自动化研究者是一次性的 S 曲线，不是递归自我改进；假设变便宜后，湿实验、GPU 与工厂才变成稀缺互补品，因此超级智能离不开物理世界。

Bob McGrew, CEO and co-founder of Arda, formerly an early Palantir exec and OpenAI's chief research officer, argues the hard-takeoff story is "straightforwardly wrong." Automating 10%, then 50%, then 99% of a researcher's time does not unlock runaway RSI, because researchers are not the bottleneck: "the amount of time it takes in order for you to discover something is not primarily bounded by the number of researchers you have." Even as CRO in 2024, the limiter was GPUs, placement, and networking. Researcher automation might move GPUs from ~35% toward ~90% of optimal discovery throughput (a big one-time gain, maybe comparable to adding reasoning), but "the difference between going from 90 to 99% of optimal... is actually not a big difference," and it does not kick off a self-improvement cycle.

Arda CEO 兼联合创始人 Bob McGrew（早年 Palantir 核心高管、曾任 OpenAI chief research officer）认为 hard takeoff 故事「straightforwardly wrong」。把研究者时间自动化到 10%、50%、再到 99%，并不会解锁失控的 RSI，因为研究者不是瓶颈：「发现东西需要的时间，主要并不受研究者人数限制。」即便 2024 年他做 CRO 时，限制因素也是 GPU、放置与网络。自动化研究或许能把 GPU 从约 35% 拉到约 90% 的最优发现吞吐（一次性大收益，或许可比肩引入 reasoning），但「从 90% 到 99% 其实差别不大」，也启动不了自我改进循环。

Look outside, he says: ten years of AI barely changed the atoms of daily life. Even a Waymo is only halfway into the physical world: the first ride feels amazing; by the third you are stuck in the same San Francisco traffic, in a car that still does not fly. When intelligence gets cheap, complements get scarce: wet labs, bulldozers, data-center buildout. Digital simulations that cut biology experiments 10x still get eaten quickly, then you bang into the physical world again. Abundance talk in Silicon Valley is a physical word; speeding only the digital half of housing does almost nothing if permits and construction dominate.

他说：往窗外看，十年 AI 几乎没改日常生活的原子。即便 Waymo 也只算半只脚踏进物理世界：第一次觉得神奇，第三次还是困在同样的旧金山拥堵里，车也不会飞。智能变便宜后，互补品变稀缺：湿实验、推土机、数据中心建设。把生物实验砍到十分之一的数字模拟会被很快吃掉，然后你又撞上物理世界。硅谷爱谈的 abundance 本身是物理词；住房若卡在许可证与施工，只加速数字那一半几乎没用。

Arda's bet is American manufacturing competitiveness through flexible automation. High-mix products (he cites iPhones changing ~75% of parts yearly) cannot amortize bespoke robot lines the way cars can over seven years; the goal is to make turning over an automated line as easy as retraining human workers, ideally uploading a product design and reconfiguring a line in about seven days. Near-term customers: defense, consumer electronics, data-center supply chains that need fast design-to-battlefield or design-to-fab cycles. Practically they partnered with Anduril (ASR also "Androl") on a manufacturing-engineering platform: single source of truth for the process plan, reconfigurable lines, first user cutting a virtual build from days to ~90 minutes, now across divisions including drones, missiles, and the Fury collaborative combat airplane. They are not training frontier models; frontier models orchestrate CAD tools and motion planning. Form factor: often one or two arms plus a camera beat full humanoids for closed-world assembly. Longer arc: factories that build factories, machines that make machines, and eventually self-replicating probes so civilization (hopefully ours) can be the light-speed wave.

Arda 的赌注是用柔性自动化让美国制造重新有竞争力。高混线产品（他举例 iPhone 每年约 75% 零件会改）无法像汽车那样用七年摊销定制机器人线；目标是让自动化产线换型像培训人工一样快，理想状态是上传产品设计、约七天重配产线。近端客户：需要快速设计-战场或设计-制造闭环的国防、消费电子、数据中心供应链。落地他们与 Anduril（ASR 也听成 Androl）合作制造工程平台：工艺计划单一事实源、可重配产线，首个用户把虚拟 build 从几天压到约 90 分钟，现已覆盖多条产品线（无人机、导弹、Fury 协同作战飞机等）。他们不训 frontier 模型；frontier 模型编排 CAD 工具与运动规划。形态上：封闭世界装配里，一两只臂加摄像头常常胜过完整人形。更长线：工厂造工厂、机器造机器，最终是自我复制探测器，让文明（希望是我们）成为以光速铺开的那一波。
