Box CEO Aaron Levie argues that multi-model agentic systems are the clear future, pointing to Cursor research where a frontier model plans and orchestrates while a cheaper workhorse model executes. The reported result is about a 15X cost improvement on total project tokens, because only a few moments in a large task need frontier intelligence: decomposition, design decisions, and hard trade-offs. Once ambiguity is collapsed into explicit instructions, cheaper models can follow them. Levie says this routing pattern is how applied AI companies will differentiate in coding, finance, legal, healthcare, and life sciences.

Box CEO Aaron Levie 认为，多模型 agentic 系统显然是未来方向。他引用 Cursor 的研究：用 frontier model 做规划与编排，再用更便宜的 workhorse model 执行，整体项目 token 成本可下降约 15 倍。因为大任务里真正需要顶尖智能的时刻不多，主要是拆解、设计决策和关键权衡；一旦歧义被收成明确指令，便宜模型就能跟着做。他说，这种按任务阶段路由模型的能力，会成为 coding、金融、法律、医疗、生命科学等应用层公司的差异化关键。

No Priors: Travel Through the Lens of AI with Booking.com CEO Glenn Fogel

The Takeaway: In travel, AI agents win by removing planning pain and raising conversion, but durable advantage still comes from continuously rebuilding products around customers, not from assuming any moat will hold.

Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel, a twenty-plus-year veteran who joined when the company was still Priceline, argues that AI is an incredible tool for making travel planning easier, cheaper, and better for both travelers and supply partners. Outside observers once assumed agentic commerce would casually erase travel incumbents; stock swings followed every OpenAI commerce rumor. Fogel's view is more grounded: complicated family trips are frustrating enough that wealthy people hire human concierges, and AI can finally give everyone a personalized agent that never forgets preferences and can search huge permutation spaces. He points to Penny, Priceline's agentic AI system, which he used for a messy multi-cabin family Europe trip; the company has said Penny adoption has been doubling monthly, with higher conversion, faster search, shorter booking paths, lower cancellations, and better customer success. The hard product work now includes token economics: which model to use for which job and when, because some steps can run on much cheaper tokens. On service, AI already picks up faster than peak-hour human queues, costs per contact are down, and satisfaction is up, but Fogel insists some customers still want a human. He also says Booking is investing roughly $700M this year across AI and broader tech enablement. The memorable line is blunt: "There is no such thing as a moat. There is no such thing as somewhere you're gonna be protected against innovation." The only long-term path is to keep inventing new services customers actually need.
