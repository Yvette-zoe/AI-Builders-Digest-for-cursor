No Priors: Chasing Trillion-Dollar Companies, Founder Ambition, Token Budgets, and Regulatory Capture with Sarah & Elad

The Takeaway: The next few years will mint plenty of large outcomes, but not a swarm of new trillion-dollar companies; the scarce skills now are honest expected-value math, founder ambition that does not flinch from the labs, and token budgets treated like capital allocation.

No Priors hosts Sarah Guo and Elad Gil (Conviction) push back on the idea that robotics, materials, and every hot vertical will mint multiple trillion-dollar firms in three to five years. Over the last half decade, a few names roughly went from near zero to a trillion; historically those arcs take fifteen to twenty years. Elad frames the moment as punctuated equilibrium: explosions, then consolidators. Coding is clearly a much bigger market than people thought, but a huge TAM is not the same as a single company getting to $50B to $100B of revenue fast enough to justify trillion-dollar talk. He also worries that some excellent founders are going niche or hardware mainly out of fear of the labs, instead of competing on product and distribution where the labs will not automatically win. On exits, he wants a non-emotional, pre-scheduled board hygiene check every six to twelve months: in AI time, one year feels like three to four. Sarah's filter is sharper: are you capturing value as costs fall and capabilities rise, or are you on the wrong side of that secular change? Inside the labs, manic timelines say code is nearly "solved" and light RSI arrives by end of next year; Sarah notes researchers have said "eighteen months to RSI" for years, and physical compute may enforce a temporary oligopoly. The management metaphor that sticks is return on invested tokens: once everyone has tried AI, the question becomes which people and projects deserve outsized token budgets. On safety and regulation, Elad reaches for nuclear and pharma history: a 1970s safety lobby helped kill abundant clean energy in the US, and Janssen-style critiques of FDA risk-without-benefit thinking are a warning if AI safety only prices downside. France still runs about 70% nuclear with none of the endlessly imagined catastrophe; the spectrum choice for AI is still open.

一句话结论：未来几年会有很多大结果，但不会突然冒出一群新的万亿公司；此刻稀缺的是诚实的期望值计算、不因大实验室而怂的 founder ambition，以及把 token budget 当资本配置来管。

No Priors 主持人 Sarah Guo 与 Elad Gil（Conviction）反驳一种流行叙事：机器人、新材料以及每个热门 vertical，都会在三到五年内批量造出多个万亿公司。过去大约五年，确实有少数公司从接近零冲到万亿市值；历史上这类曲线通常要十五到二十年。Elad 把当下看成 punctuated equilibrium：爆发，然后出现 consolidators。Coding 市场明显比原来想的大得多，但巨大 TAM 不等于一家公司能快到 $50B-$100B 收入、从而撑起万亿叙事。他还担心：一些优秀创始人主要因为怕实验室，就逃去 niche 或硬件，而不是在实验室不会自动吃掉的产品与分发上硬刚。关于 exit，他主张每六到十二个月做一次非情绪化、预排程的董事会卫生检查：在 AI 时间里，一年约等于普通周期的三到四年。Sarah 的过滤器更锋利：成本下降、能力上升时，你是在捕获价值，还是站在这轮世俗变化的错误一侧？实验室内部有种躁动时间表：代码快被“解决”，明年年底可能出现 light RSI；Sarah 提醒，研究者喊 “还有十八个月到 RSI” 已经喊了很多年，而物理算力约束反而可能暂时强化寡头竞争。最容易记住的管理隐喻是 return on invested tokens：人人都试过 AI 之后，真正的问题变成哪些人和项目该拿到超配的 token budget。安全与监管上，Elad 借用核能和制药史：1970 年代的安全游说在美国几乎杀死了充裕清洁能源；Janssen 式对 FDA “只看风险、不算收益” 的批评，也是警告：如果 AI 安全只给 downside 定价会怎样。法国核能占比仍约 70%，并没有想象中没完没了的灾难；AI 该落在光谱哪一端，仍是开放选择题。
